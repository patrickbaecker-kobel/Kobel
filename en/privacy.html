2. What this app is about

Kobel helps a household organise everyday life: tasks, shopping lists, appointments, recipes, supplies and similar things. Everything you enter is stored so that it is available on your devices and – if you invite someone to your household – to the other members of that household as well.

Important regarding the shared household: Anyone who joins your household using the join code can see and change all household data – with one exception: entries in the “Private” area are visible only to you. The password vault is shared with the household, but can only be read with the vault code.

3. What data is processed, for what purpose and on what legal basis

3.1 Account and sign-in

Your email address is processed for signing in. You receive a sign-in link; no password is stored. Your display name, an initial and a colour for display within the app are also stored.

Purpose: providing the app and associating your data with your account.
Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR (performance of the user relationship).

3.2 Content you enter yourself

Everything you create in the app is stored. Depending on which areas you use, this may include:

  • tasks, household routines, weekly planning, notes
  • shopping lists, supplies, recipes, meal planning
  • appointments, contracts and notice periods
  • trips with packing lists, visits with guest lists
  • contact details (names, phone numbers, email addresses, postal addresses)
  • vehicles (including licence plates and insurance details), pets, plants, appliances
  • numbers such as meter readings, insurance or customer numbers
  • cost sharing: who paid what and who owes whom how much
  • savings goals and purchase plans
  • profile pictures, if you select or take one

Purpose: this content is the function of the app – without it there is nothing to display.
Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR.

3.3 Health-related entries

The app has an area for medication and allows appointments in the category “Medical/check-up”. If you make entries there, these are health data within the meaning of Art. 9 GDPR – a specially protected category.

These entries are entirely voluntary. They are stored solely so that you can find them again in the app; they are not analysed and not passed on. By entering them, you expressly consent to their storage (Art. 9(2)(a) GDPR). You can delete them at any time. If you would rather not, simply do not use these areas – the rest of the app works independently of them.

3.4 Information about other people

When you create contacts, guest lists or similar, you are entering data about other people. You are responsible for that information yourself. As long as you use the app exclusively for private and family purposes, the household exemption applies to you (Art. 2(2)(c) GDPR). Please do not enter data about third parties that you would not want the other members of your household to see.

3.5 Activity log

When a task or routine is ticked off, the app records what was done, by whom and when – visible in the “Statistics” area. This is intentional: in a shared household it should be possible to see how the work is distributed.

Purpose: transparency within the household.
Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR.

3.6 Calendar connection

You can connect calendars – either via a public subscription link (ICS) or via a CalDAV account with iCloud or Yahoo. In the second case your Apple ID or Yahoo address and an app-specific password are stored so that appointments can be synchronised.

To be frank: these app-specific passwords are stored unencrypted in the database. They are protected by the database access rules (members of your household only) and by the hosting provider’s storage encryption – but not additionally by the app itself. App-specific passwords are designed for exactly this kind of use: they are limited in scope and can be revoked at your provider within seconds. If that is not enough for you, use a subscription link instead.

3.7 Password vault

The password vault works differently from the rest of the app: your entries are encrypted on your device before they are stored. The vault code never leaves your device. Only unreadable data is held on the server – not even I, as the operator, can decrypt it. This also means: if the vault code is forgotten, the entries cannot be recovered. There is deliberately no recovery option, because it would open the door to others as well.

4. Permissions on your device

  • Camera: to scan barcodes for supplies and to take a profile picture.
  • Photos: to select an existing image as a profile picture.
  • Face ID / Touch ID: to unlock the password vault. The app never sees the biometric features themselves – iOS handles that.
  • Notifications: for reminders about items that are due. These reminders are created and scheduled on the device; there is no push server that learns about them.

Each of these permissions is only requested when you use the corresponding feature, and you can withdraw it at any time in the iOS settings. Your language choice, appearance setting and the arrangement of your tiles are stored locally on the device only.

5. Where the data is stored

I use Supabase (Supabase, Inc.) as the technical basis for the database, sign-in, file storage and server-side functions. The project is hosted in the Frankfurt am Main (eu-central-1) region, so your data is located in the European Union. Supabase processes it as a processor on the basis of a Data Processing Addendum that forms part of the Supabase terms of service. Supabase is a US company; access from the USA in the course of maintenance and support cannot be ruled out. Such transfers are covered by the EU Standard Contractual Clauses under Art. 46(2)(c) GDPR, which are included in that agreement. The sub-processors used by Supabase can be viewed in a public list; changes are announced in advance.

The app is distributed via the App Store (Apple Distribution International Ltd., Ireland). In doing so, Apple processes its own data about downloads and usage, over which I have no influence – Apple’s privacy policy applies to that.

6. When data is passed to third parties

I do not sell data and do not pass it on for advertising. Data is shared:

  • with Supabase as a processor (see section 5),
  • with iCloud or Yahoo, if you connect a CalDAV account – appointments are then synchronised with your calendar there,
  • with the provider of an ICS subscription link that you enter yourself – that link is called during synchronisation,
  • with the other members of your household, which is of course the point of the app.

7. No tracking, no analytics, no automated decisions

The app contains no advertising or analytics components, no crash reporting and no transfer to third parties for advertising purposes. There is no automated decision-making and no profiling within the meaning of Art. 22 GDPR.

8. How long the data is kept

  • Your content is stored until you delete it or delete your account.
  • Deleted entries stay in the trash for 24 hours so that you can restore them, and are permanently removed after 30 days.
  • If you delete your account (in the app under Settings → Account), your membership including private entries and profile picture is deleted. If you are the last person with access to the household, the entire household is deleted with all its data. This cannot be undone.
  • Automatic backups are currently not set up. There is therefore no copy from which data could reappear after deletion – but equally none from which anything could be restored.

9. Your rights

Under the GDPR you have the right to:

  • access to the data stored about you (Art. 15),
  • rectification of incorrect data (Art. 16),
  • erasure (Art. 17),
  • restriction of processing (Art. 18),
  • data portability (Art. 20),
  • object to the processing (Art. 21),
  • withdraw consent (Art. 7(3)) – this concerns the health-related entries under section 3.3 in particular.

You can do much of this directly in the app: change or delete entries, delete your account. For anything else, write to Info-KobelApp@pm.me.
